RESEARCH

An arXiv paper titled ARTOO-DARTU studies augmented reality human-robot collaboration during a warehouse task, examining how AR situated analytics can obstruct important real-world elements when content is dynamically positioned relative to mobile robots, and proposing obstruction mitigation techniques to improve safety and usability.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25202

Researchers on arXiv present a co-designed preliminary repository of augmented reality concepts for real-time emotion regulation, addressing the lack of user-centered, expert-informed design resources for AR-based mental health support that does not disengage users from their ongoing activities.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25271

An arXiv study investigates dissociable spatial and temporal effects of interaction latency in virtual reality, examining how motion-to-photon latency from head movement and hand-to-virtual-effector loops impair VR performance and whether common spatial, temporal, and efficiency measures capture latency effects equivalently.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25681

An arXiv study with 60 participants finds that immersive virtual reality presented through a head-mounted display alters perceived functional body size, using a passable aperture paradigm to compare action and perception tasks across physical and VR conditions.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00824

An arXiv paper introduces a support-aware appearance method for building consistent 3D Gaussian street scenes from sparse and imperfect 2D anchors, using teacher-relative appearance residual distillation to handle view-specific noise and enable standard-rasterizer deployment.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26007

A paper posted to arXiv introduces SplatPainter, a method for interactively editing 3D Gaussian Splatting assets by propagating 2D edits into the underlying 3D representation via test-time training, addressing limitations of diffusion- and optimization-based approaches that the authors describe as slow, destructive to asset identity, or lacking fine-grained control.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05354

A new arXiv paper introduces StayStill, a large-scale 3D idle animation dataset comprising approximately six hours of motion data captured from 50 subjects, aimed at filling a gap in training data for automatic generation of realistic inactive-state behaviors in virtual characters. The authors argue that idle motion has received limited attention compared to other animation tasks despite its importance for believable avatars in XR and gaming contexts.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13693
