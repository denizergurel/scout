RESEARCH

A paper published on arXiv introduces a framework for quantifying full-body immersion in virtual experiences, proposing three tiers — audio-visual immersion, physical immersion, and kinetic immersion — as a way to evaluate how whole-body interactions reshape user perception in immersive environments.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22521

Researchers on arXiv present AnyMo, a geometry-aware modeling approach designed to learn human motion representations from wearable inertial sensors that transfer across different body locations, mounting positions, and device hardware, addressing a key limitation that has hindered cross-device motion sensing in the wild.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22715

Researchers on arXiv propose ViPS, a method for generating video-informed pose spaces for auto-rigged 3D meshes, aiming to constrain joint configurations to plausible, anatomically valid manifolds and reduce common artifacts such as hyperextension and self-intersection during pose sampling or manipulation.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17623

A new arXiv paper introduces MambaGaze, a bidirectional Mamba-based framework that estimates real-time cognitive load from eye-tracking signals while explicitly modeling missing data from blinks and tracking failures. The authors position the approach for adaptive human-centered AI in safety-critical settings such as driver vigilance monitoring and automated flight deck assistance.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22775

A new arXiv paper examines how augmented reality systems can be designed for preschoolers aged 3 to 8, a user group whose unpredictable and physically dynamic behavior strains assumptions baked into most commercial AR experiences. The authors frame young children as a stress test for mobile spatial computing and explore design implications for early-childhood AR.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27386

An arXiv paper titled EyeSpy demonstrates a side-channel attack that can infer a VR user's eye gaze by observing the effects of dynamic foveated rendering, even when direct access to gaze data is blocked. The researchers argue that current permission-based protections are insufficient on DFR-enabled headsets because gaze data is intrinsically used to drive the rendering pipeline.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27939

An arXiv paper presents PocketGS, a method for training 3D Gaussian Splatting models directly on mobile devices under minute-scale time budgets and limited memory, which the authors argue makes high-fidelity on-device scene reconstruction practical where standard 3DGS training requires workstation-level compute. The work targets the gap between real-time 3DGS rendering on phones and the heavier training step that has so far kept the pipeline tethered to desktops.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17354

A new paper by Harvard researchers describes an AI-driven smartphone application with personalized audio guidance designed to improve outdoor navigation for people with blindness or moderate-to-severe visual impairment, based on a survey of 112 users identifying turn-by-turn directions, path guidance, and obstacle detection as essential features. The work highlights how on-device AI is extending accessibility tools beyond traditional aids like white canes and guide dogs.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29120
